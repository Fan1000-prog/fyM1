\begin{abstract}
Open language models that cover Malagasy tend to answer like an official letter: formal, stiff, and often
half-translated from French or English. People in Madagascar do not text like that. We fine-tune an open
model, \fyBaseModel{} \citep{afriqueqwen}, with QLoRA on a single free T4 GPU so that it replies the way a
friend would: short, friendly, mixing in French, and using everyday spelling. The training mix combines
real family chats that passed a strict automatic anonymisation gate, synthetic chats from an open
permissively licensed model, short formal chats built from Malagasy Wikipedia, and identity chats written
by the data owner. On \fyEvalPrompts{} fresh everyday prompts we report the share of replies that drift
into full French or English (base: \fyDriftBase{}; fy-m1: \fyDriftFymOne{}), blind LLM-judge scores, and
regression checks on reading (Belebele) and translation (FLORES+). No private text is released. A blind
human comparison reports early votes here; the full panel's result comes in v0.2.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Malagasy is the national language of Madagascar, but most Malagasy text online is formal: news,
Wikipedia, religious and government writing. Models trained on that text learn that register. When a
user writes a short, casual message, they get back a polished paragraph that no one would send to a
friend.

Everyday Malagasy looks different. French words are mixed in freely, including French verbs used with
Malagasy grammar (``lancer-na''). Spelling is phonetic and shortened (``zany'' for ``izany''). Replies are
short, with humour and emoji. For this project that mixed style is the target, not an error: a reply
only counts as drifting if it is entirely French or English with no Malagasy in it.

Our goal is modest and measurable: a model that is clearly better than its base model at everyday
Malagasy chat, without losing much general Malagasy ability, trained and served at zero cost, and
released under a simple open licence. We contribute (i)~a base-model comparison for Malagasy chat on free
hardware, (ii)~a privacy-first pipeline that turns a family's chat export into training data without
anyone reading the text, and (iii)~fy-m1 itself, with every number in this paper written by a script from
measured outputs.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Malagasy in multilingual models.} Malagasy is covered by large multilingual benchmarks such as
Belebele \citep{bandarkar-etal-2024-belebele} and FLORES \citep{goyal-etal-2022-flores,nllb2022}, now
maintained as FLORES+ \citep{maillard-etal-2024-findings,floresplus}. Recent open models list Malagasy
among their languages, including the Gemma family \citep{gemma3,gemma4} and AfriqueQwen, a Qwen-based
model adapted to African languages \citep{afriqueqwen}. These benchmarks measure formal reading and
translation; none measures casual chat.

\paragraph{Parameter-efficient fine-tuning.} LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} learns small low-rank adapters on a
frozen model; QLoRA \citep{dettmers2023qlora} keeps the frozen model in 4-bit, so a model of about ten
billion parameters trains on one 16\,GB GPU. We use Unsloth \citep{unsloth} for speed and memory.

\paragraph{Evaluation and compression.} LLM judges are a cheap proxy for human preference
\citep{zheng2023judging}; we use one blind and treat it as a signal, not a verdict. Language ID uses
GlotLID \citep{kargaran-etal-2023-glotlid}, translation uses chrF++ \citep{popovic-2017-chrf}.
Vocabulary trimming follows \citet{ushio-etal-2023-efficient}; layer pruning
\citep{gromov2024deeper} is left for later.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
We describe the data but do not share it: part of it is private conversations. Only counts are
published (\path{results/data_stats.json}, \path{results/data_gate.json}).

\paragraph{Real chats.} The main source is one family's Messenger export (threads used: \fyDataThreads{}). All
participants consented to the use of their messages, and every sender's side is used as the assistant
in turn. The export never leaves the data owner's laptop. A local script splits it into short
multi-turn conversations on long gaps, merges back-to-back messages from the same sender, and replaces
names, nicknames (also inside message text and handles), phone numbers, emails and links with fakes or
placeholders (\path{docs/anonymisation.md}).

\paragraph{Strict automatic gate.} No person reads the cleaned text; a strict automatic gate replaces the
owner review we first planned. Of \fyGateBefore{} cleaned conversations, it dropped \fyGateDropped{}
(a conversation can count under more than one reason):
\fyGateDroppedNumber{} for a long digit run (a possible phone number), \fyGateDroppedContact{} for an
email, link or handle, \fyGateDroppedUnknown{} for too many unknown words and \fyGateDroppedFakes{} for
needing more than five fake names. A word is kept only if a known-word list has it: common Malagasy
words, Malagasy Wikipedia vocabulary \citep{wikipedia-dump}, Malagasy web text from HPLT 2.0
\citep{hplt2}, a short hand list of places and brands (\texttt{known\_names}), and French and English
words from \texttt{wordfreq} \citep{wordfreq}. French first names
from INSEE \citep{insee-prenoms} never count as known words. Any other word is replaced with a fake
name; in a name position (mid-sentence or after a person marker) only the hand list can clear a word. This over-masks rare
words on purpose: \fyGateReplaced{} words were replaced in total, a median of \fyGateFakesMedian{} and at
most \fyGateFakesMax{} per kept conversation. \fyGateKept{} conversations (\fyDataKeptMessages{}
messages) passed.

\paragraph{Synthetic chats.} Real chats are far too few on their own, so an open model run by us writes
everyday chats, few-shot prompted with cleaned real chats. Only permissively licensed generators are
allowed, because synthetic chats are training data and their licence reaches fy-m1: the code refuses any
other. The generator was \fySynthGenerator{} (licence \fySynthLicence{}); an automatic filter kept
\fySynthKept{} of \fySynthRaw{} generated chats, and an automatic sample gate checked them before use.
No closed-API model output is in the training data.

\paragraph{Formal slice.} \fyDataFormal{} short ``tell me about'' chats built from Malagasy Wikipedia
paragraphs, so facts and standard spelling survive. The slice is kept small so it does not pull the
style back to formal.

\paragraph{Identity chats.} \fyDataIdentity{} short exchanges, written by the data owner in their own
words, answer ``who are you?'' consistently (fy-m1, made by Fan's lab, built on an open model) and
politely refuse harmful requests.

In total the model trained on \fyDataTrainExamples{} examples (\fyDataSynthetic{} synthetic).

\section{Choosing the base model}
\label{sec:selection}
We compared open instruction models that fit QLoRA training on a free 16\,GB T4 GPU. Each candidate was
loaded in 4-bit and scored on: reading comprehension (Belebele, \texttt{plt\_Latn}), both as plain-text
0-shot completion (one protocol for every candidate) and in the model's chat format; translation in both
directions with English (FLORES+ devtest, chrF++); tokens per Malagasy word; a short QLoRA smoke run on a
T4 (seconds per step); and a blind greeting gate, in which the judge of Section~\ref{sec:eval} scored
\fyGreetAnswers{} everyday greeting answers per model with model names hidden. The owner's rule sets the
licence: fy-m1 ships under a simple open licence, so the base must have one.

Table~\ref{tab:selection} shows the measured values (\path{results/model_selection.json},
\path{results/greeting_gate.json}). We chose \fyBaseModel{} (licence \fyBaseLicense{}). It has the
best English$\to$Malagasy translation of the models measured so far; Gemma~4's score is pending (chrF++ \fySelQwenChrfEnMg{}, versus \fySelGemmaThreeChrfEnMg{}
for Gemma~3 12B; Gemma~4 12B: \fySelGemmaFourChrfEnMg{}). On the greeting gate it ties Gemma~4 12B
(judge mean \fyGreetQwenMean{} versus \fyGreetGemmaFourMean{}; Malagasy replies \fyGreetQwenMalagasy{}
versus \fyGreetGemmaFourMalagasy{}), and chat-format Belebele is equal within noise
(\fySelQwenBelebeleChat{} versus \fySelGemmaFourBelebeleChat{}). Its T4 smoke ran in 16-bit at
\fySelQwenSecStep{} seconds per step (Gemma~3: \fySelGemmaThreeSecStep{}; Gemma~4:
\fySelGemmaFourSecStep{}, expected to be slower because it likely needs 32-bit on a T4), which saves free GPU time.
We did not wait for Gemma~4's FLORES+ and smoke runs; it stays a comparison row.

Gemma~3 12B \citep{gemma3} is a measured baseline only: its terms of use would make fy-m1 a Gemma
derivative, which rules it out under the licence rule (greeting judge mean \fyGreetGemmaThreeMean{}).
AfriqueQwen-8B was dropped early: it is a base model with no chat-tuned twin. A known risk of the chosen
base is one reply in Hausa and some prompt echoes in its greeting answers; training on everyday chats
targets exactly that.

\section{Training}
\label{sec:method}
We train with QLoRA \citep{dettmers2023qlora}: the base model is frozen in 4-bit NF4 and we learn LoRA
adapters \citep{hu2022lora} on all linear layers, with rank \fyLoraR{} and alpha \fyLoraAlpha{}, using
Unsloth \citep{unsloth} on a free Kaggle GPU (\fyTrainGpu{}), with Colab's free tier as a backup.
Settings: learning rate \fyLearningRate{} with a \fyLrScheduler{} schedule, sequence length
\fyMaxSeqLen{}, effective batch size \fyEffectiveBatch{}, \fyTrainEpochs{} epochs (\fyTrainSteps{} steps,
\fyTrainTime{} of training). Loss is computed on assistant turns only, in the base model's own chat
template with thinking turned off; every assistant turn carries the same empty thinking block the model
sees when answering. A fixed system prompt in Malagasy names the model. Free sessions disconnect, so
checkpoints go to a private repository during training and a new session resumes from the latest one;
a run refuses to resume if its settings or code changed. Training code reaches the GPU only as a synced
copy of the clean main branch. Early stopping uses the repository's own tested rule. Final training loss
was \fyTrainLoss{}; best held-out loss was \fyEvalLoss{}.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
We generated answers from the base model and fy-m1 to \fyEvalPrompts{} fresh everyday prompts with the
same system prompt and sampling settings (Table~\ref{tab:results}). The prompts were written fresh, not
taken from the chats, so the test set holds no private text and is public (\path{eval/prompts.jsonl}).

\paragraph{Drift.} A reply counts as Malagasy if GlotLID \citep{kargaran-etal-2023-glotlid} says so, or
if at least a tenth of its words are common Malagasy words; mixed Malagasy--French passes. A reply that
is fully French or English drifts.

\paragraph{LLM judge.} An LLM judge scored both answer sets blind, with labels hidden and order shuffled,
on a published 1--5 rubric for naturalness and correctness \citep{zheng2023judging}. Methods disclosure:
the judge is \fyJudgeModel{}; rubric in \path{src/fym1/eval/judge_rubric.md}. The greeting gate of
Section~\ref{sec:selection} used the same rubric with \fyGreetJudge{}. Judge scores are a cheap signal, not a substitute for native
speakers.

\paragraph{Regression checks.} Belebele accuracy, FLORES+ chrF++ in both directions, and held-out chat
perplexity should not get much worse after fine-tuning.

\paragraph{Leak gate.} Any fy-m1 answer that shares a run of 8 words with the real training chats blocks
release. \fyLeakFymOne{} of fy-m1 answers had such a run; there were \fyLeakMatches{} distinct matched
runs, of which \fyLeakGeneric{} were judged generic phrases and \fyLeakRejected{} were rejected. The check
is strict and automatic: only the matched words are looked at, never the chat around them, and any
rejected run means fixing the data, filtering or retraining. An automatic identity check also covers the
identity and safety answers.

\paragraph{Blind human comparison (early votes).} Native speakers rate pairs of answers in a public
``arena'': same prompt, two unlabelled answers in random order, pick the more natural one. These are the
early votes in at release: \fyArenaVotes{} votes from \fyArenaRaters{} raters; fy-m1 win rate
\fyArenaWinRate{} (95\% Wilson interval \fyArenaCiLow{}--\fyArenaCiHigh{}; \citealp{wilson1927}).
With so few raters this is a first signal, not a result. The full panel's numbers come in v0.2.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} The test prompts, rubric, code and notebooks are public. Version 0.1
releases only aggregate scores; the per-prompt answers of both models come in v0.2, after the human
comparison closes, so raters cannot tell which model wrote which answer. The real training chats stay
private, so training can be rerun only on other data.

\section{Making the model smaller: vocabulary trimming}
\label{sec:trim}
Much of a multilingual model's vocabulary is for scripts and languages fy-m1 never uses. Following
\citet{ushio-etal-2023-efficient}, we keep only the tokens seen in Malagasy, French and English text,
plus all special and byte-fallback tokens, and drop the matching embedding and output rows. The trim
ships only if it passes three gates: held-out Malagasy text round-trips through the new tokenizer
unchanged; greedy answers are unchanged for at least 95\% of the test prompts; and Belebele stays within
one point. Result: gates \fyTrimGates{}. Vocabulary \fyVocabBefore{} $\to$ \fyVocabAfter{} tokens;
parameters \fyParamsBefore{} $\to$ \fyParamsAfter{}; download \fyGgufBefore{} $\to$ \fyGgufAfter{}; speed
on the demo CPU \fyTpsBefore{} $\to$ \fyTpsAfter{} tokens/s. Removing whole layers
\citep{gromov2024deeper} would save more but needs healing with extra training that free GPU time cannot
pay for; we leave it for v0.2.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The real chats come from one family. The style reflects their everyday speech, not every region
        or dialect, and most of the volume is synthetic.
  \item Synthetic chats inherit the generator's habits and mistakes; the automatic sample gate is not a
        native speaker.
  \item The strict gate over-masks: rare words and texting spellings get replaced with fake names, which
        makes the real chats a little less natural.
  \item The human comparison has only early votes from \fyArenaRaters{} raters. Until the full panel is
        in, the main evidence is automatic.
  \item The drift metric can be a little low: English that contains a Malagasy name or a quoted Malagasy
        word can pass as Malagasy.
  \item An LLM judge may not understand Malagasy slang well; its scores can be wrong in either direction.
  \item The model can be slow on a free CPU, get facts wrong, and invent things. We train once or twice
        on free hardware, so settings are not carefully tuned.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ethics, consent and privacy}
Everyone in the conversations consented to their messages being used. The raw export never left the
data owner's laptop; no person read the cleaned text, and only conversations that passed the strict gate
were used. The cleaned data lives in a private dataset and is not released; nor are raw answers until
v0.2. Before release, the leak gate checks that the model does not repeat training text. The anonymiser
has known limits, listed in \path{docs/anonymisation.md} and pinned as expected-failure tests: names
of people outside the chat typed as plain text, a name that is also an everyday word at the start of a
sentence, phone numbers written in words or split over two messages, and some addresses and place
names. The strict gate and the leak gate are the backstops for these. The model was trained with a
small set of polite refusals and gets a basic safety check before release, but it is not a
safety-tested product.
