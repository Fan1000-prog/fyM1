\begin{abstract}
% TODO(final): add one sentence on the main result once results/eval/ is in.
Open language models that cover Malagasy tend to answer like an official letter: formal, stiff, and often
half-translated from French or English. People in Madagascar do not text like that. We fine-tune an open
model, \fyBaseModel{}, with QLoRA on a single free GPU so that it replies the way a friend would: short,
friendly, mixing in French, and using everyday spelling. We describe how we chose the base model, how we
built and anonymised the training data, how we trained and trimmed the model, and what we measured. On
\fyEvalPrompts{} fresh everyday prompts, we report the share of replies that drift into full French or
English (base: \fyDriftBase{}; fyM1: \fyDriftFymOne{}), LLM-judge scores, and regression checks on
reading and translation. A blind human comparison is still collecting votes and is reported in v0.2.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation: the formal-versus-everyday gap}
% TODO(final): one short paragraph with an example pair from eval/prompts.jsonl (public prompts only).
Malagasy is the national language of Madagascar, but most Malagasy text online is formal: news,
Wikipedia, religious and government writing. Models trained on that text learn that register. When a
user writes a short, casual message, they get back a polished paragraph that no one would send to a friend.

Everyday Malagasy looks different. French words are mixed in freely, including French verbs used with
Malagasy grammar. Spelling is phonetic and shortened. Replies are short, with humour and emoji. For this
project, that mixed style is the target, not an error. A reply only counts as drifting if it is entirely
French or English with no Malagasy in it.

Our goal is modest and measurable: a model that is clearly better than its base model at everyday
Malagasy chat, without losing much general Malagasy ability, trained and served at zero cost.

\section{Choosing the base model}
\label{sec:selection}
We shortlisted recent open-weight instruction models between 7B and 12B parameters that fit QLoRA
training on a 16\,GB T4 GPU. Each candidate was loaded in 4-bit and scored on:
reading comprehension (Belebele, \texttt{plt\_Latn}; \citealp{bandarkar-etal-2024-belebele});
translation in both directions with English (FLORES+ devtest, chrF++;
\citealp{goyal-etal-2022-flores,nllb2022,maillard-etal-2024-findings,popovic-2017-chrf});
tokenizer efficiency (tokens per Malagasy word on Wikipedia sentences);
and a short everyday-chat sniff test rated by a native speaker (1--5).
License was a tie-breaker: we prefer permissive licenses when quality is close.
Table~\ref{tab:selection} shows the numbers. We chose \fyBaseModel{} (license: \fyBaseLicense{}).

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
We describe the data but do not share it. It contains private conversations.

\paragraph{Real chats.} The main source is one native speaker's Messenger export, using both sides of each
conversation. We parsed \fyDataThreads{} threads into \fyDataConversations{} short multi-turn
conversations, splitting on long gaps and merging back-to-back messages from the same side of the
conversation. We kept conversations where at least half of the turns are Malagasy or Malagasy--French (language ID with GlotLID; \citealp{kargaran-etal-2023-glotlid}).
A native speaker reviewed samples and approved \fyDataApproved{} conversations.

\paragraph{Formal slice.} \fyDataFormal{} short single-turn examples built from Malagasy Wikipedia, so
facts and standard spelling survive. We keep this slice small so it does not pull the style back to formal.

\paragraph{Identity and refusals.} \fyDataIdentity{} short exchanges, written by the native speaker,
that answer ``who are you?'' consistently and politely refuse harmful requests.

\paragraph{Synthetic chats.} \fyDataSynthetic{} synthetic conversations. We only planned to add them if
there were too few real chats; any synthetic chats come from an open-weight model run by us, never a
closed API, and a native speaker checks a sample.

In total the model trained on \fyDataTrainExamples{} examples.

\paragraph{Privacy process.} Raw exports never left the owner's laptop. A local script replaced names
and nicknames (also inside message text), phone numbers, emails, links and account handles before
anything was uploaded. Only the cleaned, approved data went to a private dataset repository, used by
the training notebook and never made public. Test prompts were written fresh, so the test set contains
no private text and is public.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
We train with QLoRA \citep{dettmers2023qlora}: the base model is frozen in 4-bit NF4 and we learn LoRA
adapters \citep{hu2022lora} on all linear layers, with rank \fyLoraR{} and alpha \fyLoraAlpha{}. We use
Unsloth \citep{unsloth} on a free Google Colab GPU (\fyTrainGpu{}). Settings: learning rate
\fyLearningRate{} with a cosine schedule, sequence length \fyMaxSeqLen{}, effective batch size
\fyEffectiveBatch{}, \fyTrainEpochs{} epochs (\fyTrainSteps{} steps, \fyTrainTime{} of training).
Loss is computed on assistant turns only, in the base model's own chat template, with a fixed system
prompt in Malagasy. Free sessions disconnect, so checkpoints are pushed to a private repository during
training and a new session resumes from the latest one. Final training loss was \fyTrainLoss{}; best
held-out loss was \fyEvalLoss{}.

\section{Making the model smaller: vocabulary trimming}
\label{sec:trim}
Much of a multilingual model's vocabulary is for scripts and languages fyM1 never uses. Following
\citet{ushio-etal-2023-efficient}, we keep only the tokens seen in Malagasy, French and English text,
plus all special and byte-fallback tokens, and drop the matching embedding and output rows.
The trim ships only if it passes three gates: held-out Malagasy text round-trips through the new
tokenizer unchanged; greedy answers to the test prompts are unchanged for almost all prompts; and
Belebele stays within one point. Result: gates \fyTrimGates{}. Vocabulary \fyVocabBefore{} $\to$
\fyVocabAfter{} tokens; parameters \fyParamsBefore{} $\to$ \fyParamsAfter{}; download \fyGgufBefore{}
$\to$ \fyGgufAfter{}; speed on the demo CPU \fyTpsBefore{} $\to$ \fyTpsAfter{} tokens/s.

Removing whole layers \citep{gromov2024deeper} would save more, but needs healing with extra training
that free GPU time cannot pay for. We leave it for v0.2.

\paragraph{Automatic judge.} An LLM judge scored both answer sets blind, with labels hidden and order
shuffled, on a published 1--5 rubric for naturalness and correctness
\citep{zheng2023judging}. Judge: \fyJudgeModel{}. Judge scores are a cheap signal, not a substitute for native speakers.

\paragraph{Leak check.} We test whether fyM1 repeats training text: \fyLeakFymOne{} of its answers share
an 8-word sequence with the training chats. The release gate was at most 1\%.

\paragraph{Blind human comparison (pending, v0.2).} Native speakers rate pairs of answers in a public
``arena'': same prompt, two unlabelled answers in random order, pick the more natural one. So far:

interval \fyArenaCiLow{}--\fyArenaCiHigh{}; \citealp{wilson1927}).

\section{Limitations}
% TODO(final): add anything the results show (weak categories, judge/human disagreement).
\begin{itemize}
  \item The style comes mostly from one person's chats and their contacts. It reflects
        Antananarivo-style everyday speech, not other regions or dialects.
  \item The human comparison is still running. Until it is in, the main evidence is automatic.
  \item An LLM judge may not understand Malagasy slang well; its scores can be wrong in either direction.
  \item The model is small and runs on a free CPU. It can be slow, get facts wrong, and invent things.
  \item We train only once or twice on free hardware, so settings are not carefully tuned.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ethics, consent and privacy}
The data owner confirmed that everyone in the conversations consented to their messages being used.
Raw data never left the owner's machine; only anonymised, approved data was used for training, and it is
not released. We check that the model does not repeat training text before release (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
The model was trained with a small set of polite refusals and gets a basic safety check before release,
but it is not a safety-tested product. No closed-API model outputs are in the training data.
